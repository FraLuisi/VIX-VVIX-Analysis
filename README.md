# VIX–VVIX Predictability

### Does the VVIX contain incremental predictive information for forecasting the VIX?

This repository contains the empirical analysis developed for my Master's thesis in Quantitative Finance.

The study investigates whether the **VVIX provides incremental predictive information for the VIX beyond the information already contained in the historical dynamics of the VIX**.

The analysis combines the study of VIX–VVIX dynamics with an out-of-sample forecasting exercise based on the **Heterogeneous Autoregressive (HAR) model**.

---

## Research Design

The empirical analysis consists of two parts.

**1. VIX–VVIX Dynamics**

The first part examines the statistical properties and relationship between the VIX and VVIX, including:

- Descriptive statistics and distributional properties
- Stationarity and mean reversion
- Rolling VIX–VVIX correlations
- Lead–lag relationships
- Granger causality

**2. VIX Forecasting**

The second part compares a benchmark HAR model with an extended HAR-VVIX specification across four forecast horizons:

- 1 trading day
- 5 trading days
- 10 trading days
- 22 trading days

Forecast performance is evaluated out of sample using **RMSE, MAE, Clark–West and Diebold–Mariano tests**.

The analysis also examines forecast performance during the **Global Financial Crisis** and the **COVID-19 crisis**.

---

## Main Findings

The results provide a qualified answer to the research question:

- The VVIX contains some incremental predictive information beyond historical VIX dynamics.
- Its average out-of-sample forecasting contribution is small and does not lead to systematic forecast superiority over the HAR benchmark.
- The relative contribution of the VVIX becomes more favourable at longer forecast horizons.
- Forecast differences become substantially larger during selected phases of financial stress.
- Overall, the predictive contribution of the VVIX appears to be **state- and horizon-dependent rather than constant over time**.

The results also suggest that the predictive contribution of the VVIX may become more pronounced when the contemporaneous VIX–VVIX relationship weakens. This relationship is suggestive and is not formally established in the thesis.

---

## Methodology

The empirical framework includes:

- Augmented Dickey–Fuller tests
- AR(1) mean-reversion analysis
- Rolling correlations
- Lead–lag cross-correlations
- Granger causality tests
- HAR and HAR-VVIX regressions
- HAC/Newey–West inference
- Rolling out-of-sample forecasting
- RMSE and MAE forecast evaluation
- Clark–West tests
- Diebold–Mariano tests
- Crisis-period analysis

The forecasting models are estimated for horizons of **1, 5, 10 and 22 trading days**.
